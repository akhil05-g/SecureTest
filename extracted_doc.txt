ProctorIQ
AI-Powered Assessment Integrity Engine
Monitor everyone. Review only the suspicious few.
1. Executive Summary
ProctorIQ is a company-facing AI-powered assessment integrity platform for organizations conducting large-scale online coding, aptitude, technical, and recruitment assessments. Its primary users are HR teams, assessment leads, and authorized reviewers. Candidates experience the normal assessment interface, while the internal integrity engine analyzes permitted monitoring signals in the background.
The core problem is scale: hundreds or thousands of candidates can generate thousands of monitoring events and hours of recordings. ProctorIQ detects events, assigns confidence and severity, correlates behavior over time, calculates cumulative risk, applies configurable thresholds, and stores relevant evidence so the lead can focus on the small number of sessions requiring attention.
2. Problem Statement
HR and assessment leads cannot realistically watch complete recordings for every candidate.
Individual alerts can create alert fatigue and lack context.
A single harmless event should not automatically be treated as cheating.
Repeated or combined events can be more meaningful than isolated events.
Reviewers need exact timestamps and evidence when investigating a flag.
Different assessments require different integrity policies and thresholds.
3. Product Concept
ProctorIQ changes the workflow from 'detect everything and make HR review everything' to 'monitor everyone, intelligently prioritize the suspicious few.' It is an assessment-integrity intelligence layer rather than simply another webcam recorder.
The system converts monitoring signals into structured events, correlates them, computes a risk score, and escalates candidates who cross a configurable threshold. The lead receives a concise evidence timeline rather than searching through an entire recording.

5. Candidate-Side Experience
The candidate-facing experience remains focused on taking the assessment. Internal monitoring analytics, risk scores, detection details, and the HR dashboard are not exposed as part of the candidate workflow. Monitoring permissions and organizational policies still need to be handled transparently and lawfully by the company.
6. Detection Signals
Face presence and temporary face absence.
Multiple-person / multiple-face detection.
Repeated gaze or head-position deviations.
Potential phone or prohibited-object detection.
Tab/window switching and fullscreen exits where technically and legally permitted.
Suspicious audio or external voice activity.
Relevant screen activity and anomaly signals.
Assessment-specific events that can be added later.
Each detector produces a signal rather than a final cheating verdict. Events should contain candidate ID, assessment ID, timestamp, duration, event type, severity, confidence, and evidence reference where applicable.
7. Confidence vs. Risk
ProctorIQ separates detector confidence from overall candidate risk. A detector can be highly confident that an object resembles a phone, while overall risk still depends on other events and context.
Detection confidence: confidence of a specific model/detector.
Event severity: importance under the assessment policy.
Cumulative risk: combined score based on events and context.
Threshold status: whether configured action conditions are met.
8. Cumulative Risk Engine
The risk engine considers severity, confidence, repetition, duration, timing, and combinations of independent signals. A simplified conceptual model is: Risk Score = Σ(event severity × detection confidence) + repetition/context adjustments.
One tab switch may have low impact. Repeated tab switching combined with phone detection, multiple-face detection, and external voice activity should substantially increase risk. Exact weights are intentionally not finalized and should be configurable.
9. Automatic Threshold and Flagging
A configurable threshold lets the system automatically escalate a candidate without requiring the lead to inspect every event. For example, if the threshold is 70, crossing it can move the candidate into a high-risk or auto-flagged state according to the organization's policy.
Normal: low accumulated risk.
Suspicious: unusual activity requiring prioritization.
High Risk: cumulative score is high.
Auto-Flagged: configured threshold and conditions are satisfied.
Human Override: authorized lead can confirm, dismiss, or override where policy requires it.
For responsible deployment, automated flags should be treated as integrity actions or disqualification recommendations unless organizational policy and applicable rules explicitly allow automated enforcement. Audit trails should be preserved.
10. Cloud Evidence Storage
Relevant evidence is stored securely in the cloud so the assessment lead can investigate a specific event without reviewing the entire session.
Candidate and assessment identifiers.
Timestamp and duration.
Event type, severity, and confidence.
Relevant screen snapshot or evidence segment.
Relevant webcam segment where permitted.
Relevant audio segment where permitted.
Nearby event sequence for context.
Risk score before and after the event.
Model/policy version used.
Production storage should use strong access control, encryption, retention policies, and audit logging. The cloud provider is intentionally not finalized.
11. Evidence Timeline
A central UI concept is a timeline of meaningful incidents rather than a long recording as the primary interface.
Example: 00:14:21 Tab switch; 00:22:47 Face absent; 00:31:09 Multiple faces; 00:45:17 Phone detected; 01:02:44 Tab switch; 01:14:32 Threshold crossed.
Selecting an incident should take the reviewer directly to the corresponding evidence window and show confidence, severity, risk contribution, and context.

13. End-to-End Workflow
Company creates an assessment.
Lead configures integrity rules and thresholds.
Candidates take the normal online assessment.
Permitted monitoring signals are collected.
Detection services identify events.
Events are normalized with confidence, severity, timestamp, and duration.
Correlation evaluates repetition, timing, and combinations.
Risk engine updates the cumulative score.
Threshold engine escalates candidates when conditions are met.
Relevant evidence is securely stored.
Dashboard prioritizes candidates requiring attention.
Authorized reviewers inspect evidence and record decisions.
14. High-Level Architecture
The technology stack is deliberately not finalized.
Conceptual flow: Candidate Assessment → Monitoring/Event Collection → Detection Services → Event Normalization → Event Correlation → Risk/Threshold Engine → Evidence Storage → HR/Lead Dashboard.
The system should remain modular so AI models, databases, cloud services, and application frameworks can be changed later.

16. Hackathon Demo Story
Simulate a company conducting a large online assessment. Normal candidates generate ordinary events while a smaller set generates repeated or correlated suspicious events. The dashboard updates risk scores.
At the end, the lead sees only the high-risk group. Open one candidate, show the cumulative risk and detector confidence values, click an incident on the timeline, and immediately display the relevant evidence.
Core story: 1,000 candidates → thousands of raw events → a small number of high-risk candidates → targeted evidence review.

24. Trust, Privacy, Security and Auditability
Treat AI detections as probabilistic signals, not unquestionable truth.
Keep detection confidence separate from overall risk.
Support authorized human review/override for high-impact decisions according to policy.
Restrict evidence access to authorized personnel.
Encrypt evidence and apply retention rules.
Provide required notices and permissions.
Evaluate false positives and fairness.
Record model and policy versions for incident reconstruction.
Use synthetic data for the hackathon demo wherever real candidate data is unnecessary.

27. Hackathon Application Description
We’ll build an AI-powered Assessment Integrity Engine for companies conducting large-scale online assessments. Unlike existing proctoring systems that primarily generate isolated alerts and leave HR teams to review long recordings, ProctorIQ correlates webcam, screen, browser, and audio signals into cumulative risk scores with configurable violation thresholds. Significant incidents are confidence-scored, timestamped, and securely stored as evidence, allowing assessment leads to review only the highest-risk candidates and jump directly to where suspicious behavior occurred. LatentCode will accelerate development of the real-time detection, event-correlation, risk-scoring, and evidence pipeline, while SkillPatch can provide reusable agent skills and a path for us to create and share ProctorIQ-specific development capabilities.
